01

Why the plant is at Wusuta

Cassava starts to spoil within a day or two of being pulled from the ground. Building the plant where the crop grows is what turns a perishable root into something that keeps for a year.

timer

Roots washed within 24 hours

Fast enough to stop spoiling before it starts.

Every hour a root spends in the heat costs starch and raises cyanide. Because the plant sits in the middle of the growing area, roots lifted at sunrise are in our tanks by early afternoon.

water

Next to the fish farms

Lake Volta's cage farms are on our doorstep.

Tilapia and catfish farms on the lake get through a lot of feed. Supplying them from Wusuta rather than Accra cuts the haulage, and that saving goes straight to the farmer.

02

Three things we commit to

We would rather be judged on written limits and signed contracts than on a good sales pitch.

fact_check
One

We put the spec in writing

No guesswork on grade. Every sale is against limits you can hold us to: moisture at or under 10%, an even particle size, and nothing added — no bleach, no fillers.

How you check Moisture, cyanide and particle size results go out with every shipment.
handshake
Two

We sign contracts with farmers

We do not wait for harvest to push the price down. Our farmers sign for twelve months, with a floor price, a delivery calendar, and a guarantee that we buy an agreed quantity.

Why it matters Farmers are not forced to sell cheap at harvest, and our mill keeps running.
engineering
Three

We hire and train locally

Our technicians, press operators and lab staff come from Wusuta and the surrounding towns, and are trained here on the machines they run.

Who works here Most of our technical staff live in the Volta Region.
